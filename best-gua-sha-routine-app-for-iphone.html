Best Gua Sha Routine App for iPhone: A 14-Day Plan and How the Options Compare

Updated October 2026 · By the Drena developer

Short answer. Look for an app that shows stroke direction, tells you each day which routine to do and is honest about results. Drena has a 14-Day Gua Sha program on iPhone: a few minutes a day with a stone, with routines for the jawline, forehead, neck and collarbone, under-eyes and the full face, plus rest days.

Step by step

  1. Pick the 14-Day Gua Sha programOpen Programs in Drena and choose 14-Day Gua Sha (it is also suggested first if your quiz answer was gua sha or contour).
  2. Do the routine for the dayDay 1 is the full face, day 2 the morning de-puff, day 3 the jawline, day 4 the forehead, day 5 neck and collarbone. Day 6 is a rest day.
  3. Follow the drawn pathEach stroke is drawn on the face with an arrowhead. Strokes run toward the ear and down to the collarbone, with light or feather pressure.
  4. Add progress photosTake a photo on day one and another at the end of the program to compare them yourself.
  5. Keep going or switchAfter 14 days you can start the 7-Day De-Puff or the 21-Day Face Yoga program.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a gua sha stone for the program?

The 14-Day Gua Sha program is built around a stone. Drena's other facial routines also run by hand.

How long is each session?

The gua sha routines are 3 to 5 minutes.

What does the program promise?

A de-puffed, relaxed look after each session. Drena mentions that one small trial measured millimetre changes after eight weeks, and does not promise more than that.

Are there rest days?

Yes, days 6 and 13 of the 14-Day Gua Sha program are rest days.

Is the direction really important?

Yes. Lymph moves toward nodes, so face and neck strokes route to the ear and then down to the collarbone.

Do I need an account to use Drena?

No. There is no account to create; subscription status comes from your Apple ID.

Is gua sha medically proven?

Evidence is limited and Drena grades every claim in its articles as Established, Emerging, Early or Myth. Treat gua sha as a relaxing routine, not a treatment.
